Shaping Communities

From a one-room schoolhouse to Utah's largest school district, Alpine has been woven into the fabric of Utah Valley life for over a century. These are our stories.

Shaping Communities & Building Futures

100 Years of Educating Students in Alpine School District

"It is a story of what it means to be a community."— Superintendent Vernon M. Henshaw, Ed.D.

A Timeline of 112 Years

1915–1919

The Beginning

Alpine School District was formally established in 1915, unifying schools across Lehi, American Fork, Pleasant Grove, and Orem. The district opened its doors to 4,906 students — 2,511 boys and 2,395 girls — ranging from ages 6 to 18. Students rode to school on hay bales in the backs of trucks or in horse-drawn wagons. Teachers earned as little as $55 a month, and the district immediately began advocating for student health by hiring nurses.

First student count: 4,906 across Utah Valley communities
1920–1929

Progress & Growing Pains

Leadership transitions shaped this decade. In 1922, Superintendent James H. Walker resigned and a financial crisis left 92 teachers waiting months for their salaries. Yet the district pressed forward — by the mid-1920s, staff had grown to include educational supervisors, nurses, and a dentist. The first football game in Lehi High School history was played in 1924.

First Lehi High School football game, 1924
1930–1959

Challenge, Hope & the Baby Boom

The Great Depression tested the district deeply, yet schools remained open and teachers stayed dedicated. Post-WWII, the baby boom brought explosive growth. By the 1950s, Alpine was building schools at a pace that mirrored Utah Valley's rapid expansion. School lunch programs, new bus routes, and expanded curricula defined this era of hopeful rebuilding.

District survived the Depression and emerged stronger
1960–1969

Social Change & Advancement

In 1968, out of 875 graduates, 612 went on to higher education — 40% to BYU alone. Title I legislation in 1965 expanded services to underserved students. In 1969, Alpine District assumed responsibility for special education, ensuring every child had access to a quality education. The district was recognized nationally for Utah's school consolidation model.

1968: 70% of graduates pursued higher education
1970–1979

More With Less

Student population reached 17,742 by 1970. The district launched community school programs, introduced Title IX, and opened the Clear Creek Outdoor Education Camp in 1972 — a beloved tradition still cherished by generations of Alpine students. The Spanish Immersion Program began in 1978 at Cherry Hill Elementary. First classroom computers arrived in 1978 (Commodore PET machines).

Clear Creek Camp opened 1972 — generations of memories made
1980–1989

Productivity & Innovation

Mountain View High School opened in 1980, giving Orem two high schools. In 1981, Eilene Peterson became the first female member of the ASD Board of Education. The district became one of the first in Utah to embrace classroom technology. The decade saw remarkable growth — student population climbing past 26,969 — as new cities like Highland and Cedar Hills incorporated.

First female board member, 1981
1990–1999

Keeping the Community Together

The Christa McAuliffe Space Education Center opened at Central Elementary in Pleasant Grove in 1990, captivating over 350,000 students with space simulations and teamwork missions. The decade saw continued growth and a commitment to keeping diverse communities united under one district banner as suburban growth accelerated across Utah Valley.

Space Education Center: 350,000+ students launched their imaginations
2000–2009

Unprecedented Growth

Alpine became the largest school district in Utah — and eventually moved to 47th largest in the nation out of 17,000+ districts. Student population surpassed 70,000. New cities like Saratoga Springs brought waves of new students. The district launched the American Fork High School Marching Band to national prominence, including a performance in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade.

Largest district in Utah — 68,000+ students by 2011
2010–2015

Continuous Improvement

Ten consecutive years (2003–2013) of steady increases in student test scores. Named to the National AP Honor Roll. Noelle Pikus-Pace, a Mountain View High School graduate, won a silver medal at the 2014 Winter Olympics. A culture of teacher collaboration and professional learning communities transformed outcomes across every school in the district. Despite spending barely a third of New York City's per-pupil costs, Alpine matched their outcomes.

2014: Olympian Noelle Pikus-Pace — Alpine's silver medalist
2015–2027

The Final Chapter — and a New Legacy

In 2015, Alpine School District celebrated its 100th anniversary with the publication of "Shaping Communities & Building Futures." The district continued to grow and innovate, serving communities from Lehi and Highland to Saratoga Springs and beyond. In a historic decision, Alpine School District will conclude its 112-year run in July 2027 as Utah Valley reorganizes into new districts — but the memories, the friendships, and the legacy belong to all of us forever.

April 2027: We celebrate together one final time
